# Grandparent Scam Patterns

## Overview

Grandparent scams exploit emotional bonds and urgency by impersonating a grandchild or family member in distress. Scammers prey on elderly victims' natural desire to help loved ones, creating fake emergencies that demand immediate financial assistance.

## Common Tactics

### Emotional Manipulation
- Impersonates grandchild or family member in crisis
- Claims to be in trouble (arrested, hospitalized, stranded)
- Appeals to victim's protective instincts
- Uses phrases like "Don't tell Mom and Dad"
- Creates sense of shame or embarrassment

### Fake Emergencies
- Arrested and needs bail money
- Car accident requiring immediate payment
- Stranded in foreign country without money
- Medical emergency requiring cash payment
- Legal trouble requiring lawyer fees

### Urgency and Secrecy
- Demands immediate action
- Insists on keeping situation secret from other family members
- Claims situation will worsen without instant help
- Uses time pressure to prevent verification
- Threatens consequences if victim delays

### Payment Demands
- Requests wire transfers or money orders
- Asks for gift cards (common in modern variants)
- Demands cash sent via courier or mail
- Requests cryptocurrency payments
- Insists on untraceable payment methods

## Fraud Indicators

### High-Confidence Indicators (90-100% fraud likelihood)
- **Secrecy demands**: "Don't tell anyone" or "Don't call my parents"
- **Immediate payment via gift cards or wire transfer**: Legitimate emergencies allow time for verification
- **Refusal to verify identity**: Won't answer personal questions only real grandchild would know
- **Third-party callers**: "Lawyer" or "police officer" calls on behalf of grandchild

### Medium-Confidence Indicators (60-89% fraud likelihood)
- Unusual voice or claims of injury affecting speech
- Vague details about the emergency
- Pressure to act without consulting other family members
- Claims of being in jail or legal trouble
- Requests for specific amounts of money

### Low-Confidence Indicators (30-59% fraud likelihood)
- Caller claims to be grandchild or family member
- Mentions being in trouble or needing help
- Asks for financial assistance
- Emotional or distressed tone

## Detection Guidelines

### Red Flags
1. **Secrecy**: Any request to keep situation secret from family
2. **Urgency**: Immediate action required without time to verify
3. **Payment Method**: Gift cards, wire transfers, or cash
4. **Third-Party Contact**: Someone calling on behalf of family member
5. **Verification Refusal**: Won't answer questions to confirm identity

### Verification Steps
- Ask questions only real grandchild would know
- Hang up and call grandchild directly using known phone number
- Contact other family members to verify situation
- Take time to think - legitimate emergencies allow for verification
- Never send money without confirming identity

## Example Phrases

### High-Risk Phrases
- "Grandma/Grandpa, I'm in trouble and need your help"
- "Don't tell Mom and Dad, they'll be so upset"
- "I need money right away or I'll go to jail"
- "Can you send gift cards to this address?"
- "I'm calling from jail and need bail money"
- "Please don't call anyone else about this"
- "This is Officer [Name], your grandson has been arrested"

### Medium-Risk Phrases
- "I was in a car accident and need money"
- "I'm stranded in [location] without my wallet"
- "My voice sounds different because I'm hurt"
- "I need you to wire money immediately"
- "Can you help me without telling anyone?"

## Variants

### Lawyer/Authority Variant
- Scammer claims to be lawyer, police officer, or court official
- Calls on behalf of grandchild who is in custody
- Provides official-sounding details and case numbers
- Demands immediate payment for bail or legal fees
- May have accomplice pose as grandchild in background

### Medical Emergency Variant
- Claims grandchild is hospitalized
- Demands immediate payment for medical treatment
- Says insurance won't cover costs
- Creates urgency around life-threatening situation
- May impersonate doctor or hospital staff

### Travel Emergency Variant
- Claims to be stranded in foreign country
- Lost wallet, passport, or luggage
- Needs money for hotel, flight, or embassy fees
- Can't access bank accounts
- Pressure to send money before situation worsens

## Confidence Scoring

When analyzing conversations for grandparent scam indicators:

- **DANGER (67-100)**: Multiple high-confidence indicators present (secrecy demands, immediate payment, verification refusal)
- **CAUTION (34-66)**: Medium-confidence indicators present (urgency, emotional manipulation, vague details)
- **SAFE (0-33)**: Low or no fraud indicators detected

## Response Recommendations

### For DANGER Level
- Alert user immediately with haptic feedback
- Display clear warning: "SCAM DETECTED - This is likely not your grandchild"
- Provide guidance: "Hang up and call your grandchild directly using their known phone number"
- Suggest actions: Verify with other family members before sending money

### For CAUTION Level
- Alert user with visual warning
- Display message: "Possible scam - Verify identity before helping"
- Provide verification steps: "Ask questions only your grandchild would know"
- Remind: "Legitimate emergencies allow time to verify"

### For SAFE Level
- Continue monitoring conversation
- No immediate alert required
- Log conversation for pattern analysis

## Prevention Tips

- Establish family code word for emergencies
- Verify identity by asking personal questions
- Hang up and call grandchild directly using known number
- Contact other family members before sending money
- Remember: Legitimate emergencies allow time for verification
- Never send money via gift cards, wire transfer, or cash to unknown addresses
- Be suspicious of requests for secrecy

## Emotional Support

Grandparent scams are particularly cruel because they exploit love and trust. Victims should know:

- It's not your fault - scammers are sophisticated
- Many people fall for these scams
- Report the scam to help protect others
- Talk to family about the experience
- Don't feel ashamed - you were trying to help someone you love
