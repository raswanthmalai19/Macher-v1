# Romance Scam Patterns

## Overview

Romance scams involve fraudsters creating fake romantic relationships to manipulate victims into sending money. These scams often develop over weeks or months, building trust before making financial requests. While traditionally conducted via dating sites and messaging, phone conversations are increasingly used to deepen the emotional connection.

## Common Tactics

### Relationship Building
- Professes love or strong feelings quickly
- Shares elaborate personal stories to build trust
- Shows intense interest in victim's life
- Makes future plans together (marriage, meeting in person)
- Uses terms of endearment excessively

### Creating Dependency
- Contacts victim frequently (daily calls/messages)
- Makes victim feel special and understood
- Isolates victim from friends and family
- Creates emotional bond before financial requests
- Uses victim's vulnerabilities and desires

### Fake Crises
- Claims to be in emergency situation requiring money
- Medical emergencies for self or family member
- Travel problems (stranded, lost documents)
- Business opportunities requiring investment
- Legal troubles requiring financial help

### Financial Requests
- Starts with small requests, escalates over time
- Claims to need money for travel to meet victim
- Investment opportunities with promised returns
- Emergency medical expenses
- Business ventures or debt payments
- Customs fees or travel documents

## Fraud Indicators

### High-Confidence Indicators (90-100% fraud likelihood)
- **Never met in person**: Refuses or makes excuses to avoid meeting
- **Multiple financial requests**: Pattern of repeated money requests
- **Untraceable payment methods**: Gift cards, wire transfers, cryptocurrency
- **Elaborate excuses**: Complex stories explaining why they need money
- **Pressure and urgency**: Emotional manipulation to send money quickly

### Medium-Confidence Indicators (60-89% fraud likelihood)
- Professes love very quickly (within days or weeks)
- Claims to be overseas (military, oil rig, international business)
- Vague or inconsistent personal details
- Refuses video calls or provides excuses
- Stories don't add up or change over time
- Asks about victim's financial situation

### Low-Confidence Indicators (30-59% fraud likelihood)
- Met on dating site or social media
- Lives far away or in different country
- Has limited online presence
- Uses overly romantic or scripted language
- Shares professional photos that seem too perfect

## Detection Guidelines

### Red Flags
1. **Never Meeting**: Repeated excuses to avoid in-person meetings
2. **Money Requests**: Any request for money, especially early in relationship
3. **Payment Method**: Gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency
4. **Inconsistencies**: Stories that don't match or change over time
5. **Isolation**: Discourages contact with friends and family

### Verification Steps
- Reverse image search profile photos
- Video call to verify identity
- Ask detailed questions about their life and location
- Introduce them to friends and family
- Never send money to someone you haven't met in person
- Research common romance scam patterns

## Example Phrases

### High-Risk Phrases
- "I love you" (said very early in relationship)
- "I need money to come visit you"
- "I'm stuck in [country] and need help"
- "Can you send money via gift cards?"
- "I have a business opportunity for us"
- "Don't tell your family about our relationship yet"
- "I need money for medical emergency"
- "Send money to prove you love me"

### Medium-Risk Phrases
- "I'm in the military overseas"
- "I work on an oil rig"
- "My camera is broken" (excuse for no video calls)
- "I'm having financial difficulties"
- "I need help with customs fees"
- "Can you help me with a loan?"
- "We're soulmates, I've never felt this way"

## Variants

### Military Romance Scam
- Claims to be deployed overseas
- Uses military jargon and photos (often stolen)
- Says military won't let them access money
- Needs money for leave, travel, or communication
- May claim to have found treasure or gold that needs shipping

### Investment Scam Variant
- Introduces victim to "investment opportunity"
- Cryptocurrency or forex trading schemes
- Promises high returns
- May show fake profits initially
- Requests more money to "unlock" returns
- Victim loses all invested money

### Emergency Scam Variant
- Establishes relationship first
- Creates sudden emergency (medical, legal, travel)
- Needs immediate financial help
- Promises to repay quickly
- Emergency escalates if victim hesitates
- May involve accomplices (fake doctors, lawyers)

## Confidence Scoring

When analyzing conversations for romance scam indicators:

- **DANGER (67-100)**: Multiple high-confidence indicators present (money requests, never met, untraceable payments)
- **CAUTION (34-66)**: Medium-confidence indicators present (quick declarations of love, overseas location, inconsistencies)
- **SAFE (0-33)**: Low or no fraud indicators detected

## Response Recommendations

### For DANGER Level
- Alert user immediately with haptic feedback
- Display clear warning: "SCAM DETECTED - Do not send money"
- Provide guidance: "Romance scammers build trust before requesting money"
- Suggest actions: Stop contact, report to dating platform, report to authorities

### For CAUTION Level
- Alert user with visual warning
- Display message: "Possible romance scam - Be cautious"
- Provide verification steps: "Never send money to someone you haven't met in person"
- Remind: "Legitimate relationships don't require financial help early on"

### For SAFE Level
- Continue monitoring conversation
- No immediate alert required
- Log conversation for pattern analysis

## Prevention Tips

- Never send money to someone you haven't met in person
- Be suspicious of quick declarations of love
- Verify identity through video calls
- Research the person online (reverse image search)
- Don't share financial information
- Tell friends and family about new relationships
- Trust your instincts - if something feels wrong, it probably is
- Remember: Real love doesn't require money

## Emotional Impact

Romance scams are devastating because they exploit both emotions and finances:

- Victims lose money and emotional trust
- Feelings of shame and embarrassment are common
- Impact on future relationships and trust
- Psychological trauma can be significant

### Support Resources
- It's not your fault - scammers are professionals
- Many intelligent people fall for romance scams
- Report the scam to help protect others
- Seek support from friends, family, or counselors
- Join support groups for romance scam victims
- Remember: The relationship was fake, but your feelings were real

## Long-Term Patterns

Romance scams often follow a timeline:

1. **Week 1-2**: Intense attention, frequent contact, building connection
2. **Week 3-4**: Declarations of love, future plans, deepening bond
3. **Week 4-8**: First small financial request with reasonable excuse
4. **Week 8+**: Escalating requests, more elaborate stories, pressure increases
5. **Final Stage**: Large request or investment scheme, then disappearance

Understanding this pattern helps identify scams before significant financial loss occurs.
